# DilSe 30-day publishing copy

Every caption ends with a direct action to **www.baatdilse.com**. Add the account’s standard legal or safety language only where required. Do not replace the privacy wording on day 24 with a stronger confidentiality claim.

## Day 1: Brand introduction

**Format:** Static feed post

**Caption**

Some conversations stay in your head for days because you cannot find a way to begin.

DilSe helps South Asian women think through relationship concerns, prepare a specific sentence, and practise difficult conversations before having them in real life. Start with the part you keep replaying. You do not need a finished explanation.

Visit www.baatdilse.com and start a conversation.

**Alt text:** DilSe launch card inviting South Asian women to begin a relationship conversation they have been rehearsing in their heads.

## Day 3: What do I even say?

**Format:** Reel

**Caption**

“You never help” expresses frustration, but it does not name the work that needs to change.

Try: “Can you take full responsibility for school planning this month, including notices, forms, fees, dates, and follow-up?” Specific wording gives both people something concrete to discuss.

Find your opening words at www.baatdilse.com.

**Reel script, 12 seconds**

1. 0 to 3 seconds: Show a phone note containing “You never help.” Delete the sentence.
2. 3 to 8 seconds: Type the specific school-planning request one line at a time.
3. 8 to 10 seconds: On-screen text: “Name the task. Name the owner. Include the follow-up.”
4. 10 to 12 seconds: Show the DilSe mark and www.baatdilse.com.

**Alt text:** Reel cover asking “What do I even say?” and promising to turn a vague complaint into a specific request.

## Day 4: The Listener and The Partner

**Format:** Static feed post

**Caption**

Use The Listener when you need to slow down, sort through what happened, and decide what you want to say.

Use The Partner when you want to rehearse the conversation and hear how another person might respond. You can revise the words and try again before speaking to the real person.

Try either mode at www.baatdilse.com.

**Alt text:** DilSe card explaining that The Listener helps organize thoughts and The Partner helps rehearse conversations.

## Day 16: Three ways to begin

**Format:** Reel

**Caption**

Try one of these:

“Can I tell you what has been weighing on me?”

“Could we make one decision together tonight?”

“I want to explain what I need without blaming you.”

Find an opener that sounds like you at www.baatdilse.com.

**Reel script, 15 seconds**

3. End with “Choose one. Change the words. Practise it.”

**Format:** Five-slide carousel

**Caption**

Family advice can bring experience, care, and practical help. It can also create pressure when a couple needs a different decision inside their own household.

The useful question is not whether family involvement is always good or bad. Ask which input helps and which decision the couple needs to make together.

## Day 23: Practise the difficult reply

**Format:** Reel

**Caption**

A real conversation may not follow the version you prepared.

Rehearse a supportive reply, a realistic question, and a resistant response. The purpose is not to predict the other person. It is to practise staying clear when the conversation changes direction.

Rehearse it at www.baatdilse.com.

**Reel script, 18 seconds**

1. 0 to 3 seconds: User says, “Can we agree on how family visits are decided?”
2. 3 to 7 seconds: Supportive reply: “Yes. What schedule would feel fair?”
3. 7 to 11 seconds: Realistic reply: “What is not working about the current arrangement?”
4. 11 to 15 seconds: Resistant reply: “Why does this need to change?”
5. 15 to 18 seconds: On-screen text: “Practise staying clear.” Show www.baatdilse.com.

**Alt text:** Reel cover inviting the viewer to rehearse supportive, realistic, and resistant replies.

**Caption**
